Public Sector Data Quality Commons

Is the data good enough to use, and is someone fixing it?

One free, open place for everything about data quality in the public sector: check your data in the browser, find the frameworks and standards that apply to you, learn, use AI safely, and share what works. For central and local government, the devolved governments, the wider public sector, the voluntary and private bodies that deliver public services, and the Crown Dependencies, Overseas Territories, Commonwealth and international partners the UK works with.

What is a Commons?

A commons is something a community looks after together, for everyone's benefit, like common land, Wikipedia or open-source software. Nobody owns it, everyone can use it, and the people who use it agree the rules and keep it in good shape.

Here, the commons is shared knowledge, tools and practice for data quality: open code (MIT), open content (OGL), and rules made with the people who use them, through the charter.

The mark: three circles for the many organisations that hold public data, overlapping in one shared space. At its heart, a tick for quality, the common good they share.

          Who could this miss?

          • Data that leaves out groups of people can pass every overall check. The toolkit's inclusion checks look for unknown values and for quality that differs between groups.
          • People in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are often less well served by UK data. In the NDL pilot, 45 of 48 pages covering only part of the UK didn't point to data for the other nations.
          • Data without an open, machine-readable format is harder to use with assistive technology.
          • Staff who use assistive technology must be able to run checks and work the issue log. The tooling survey asks suppliers about this.

          What this is, and isn't

          • A working prototype, built in the open as a public good: MIT-licensed code, OGL v3.0 content. It is not an official government product, and links to official guidance first.
          • Your data stays in your browser. Only what you choose to contribute is saved, and it is moderated.
          • Trust levels and AI drafts are suggestions for a person to confirm. They are not decisions.
          • See the mission and roadmap, and how to get help.
