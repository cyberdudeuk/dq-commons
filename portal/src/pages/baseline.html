How the tiers are defined

A · Measured

Rules exist for the critical data items and checks run on a schedule. A DQAP or equivalent exists. The work is mapping existing results into the return.

B · Partial

Some rules or ad hoc checks. Known issues are logged somewhere. No agreed targets and no complete baseline across dimensions.

C · Unmeasured

No rules, no profiling, and often unresolved data access, ownership or sharing-agreement questions. Data access is the biggest source of delay.

Inclusion check built into the estimate

Each CDA carries a small allowance for an equality and coverage check. Standard completeness and validity scores can hide exclusion: a record can be complete while sex, ethnicity, disability or language fields are defaulted, or while people with no fixed address or without a digital footprint never appear at all. For CDAs holding personal data, the baseline should report:

  • Completeness of protected-characteristic and need fields, separately from overall completeness
  • Use of default or "unknown" values in those fields
  • Coverage against a reference population where one exists (for example, ONS mid-year estimates)
  • Whether quality differs between groups, such as match rates by age band or postcode type

Assumptions and open questions

Assumed

  • The January return asks for a first baseline, not a complete DQAP across all 7 steps.
  • Per-activity days come from typical DQ assessment work, not from GOV.UK. The guidance gives no time estimates.
  • Rule, measurement and issue effort scale with critical data items at a power of 0.7, because rules get reused across similar fields.
  • Departments do the work; GDS runs guidance, a helpdesk and aggregation (shown as a central line).

To confirm

  • The exact fields in the CDA baseline reporting template and when it reaches departments.
  • How many CDAs were actually submitted, and the split between departments and ALBs.
  • Whether "DQAP in progress" with a dated plan is acceptable for tier C assets in January.
  • Whether CDAs shared by several organisations get one baseline (owner) or one per user.
