Accessibility statement for the Public Sector Data Quality Commons

This statement follows the structure of the GOV.UK sample accessibility statement. The Commons is a prototype; if it becomes a service run by a public body, that body will publish a statement under the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018.

We want as many people as possible to be able to use the Commons. You should be able to:

  • change colours (it follows your light or dark setting), contrast levels and fonts using browser or device settings
  • zoom in up to 400% without the text spilling off the screen
  • navigate every page using just a keyboard, including menus, tabs, tours, the carousel and forms
  • navigate using speech recognition software
  • listen to every page using a screen reader
  • stop moving content: the front page carousel has a Pause button, stops when you hover over or move into it, and doesn't move at all if you've asked your device to reduce motion

Data you check is shown as text and tables, not only as charts. Every chart has a table or text alternative.

How accessible this website is: known problems

We think the Commons is partially compliant with the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.2 AA standard, because it hasn't yet been independently audited. Things we know about:

  • There is no independent audit yet. One is planned (backlog item XDMH-213).
  • The Commons is in English only. A Welsh version is planned (XDMH-216). There is no Easy Read or British Sign Language version yet.
  • Some older pages (the National Data Library assessment, tooling research and baseline model) have dense tables that are hard to use on small screens.
  • Links to other organisations' websites, videos and podcasts go to content we don't control. We ask for captions or transcripts before we list a video or podcast.
  • Contributing, voting and answering surveys need a claude.ai sign-in, or a GitHub account for the alternative forms. We know both are barriers for some people; you can also ask for help through the service desk.

Tell us about a problem, or ask for another format

If something doesn't work for you, or you need information in a different format, use Ask the Commons for help (choose "Something else") or the service desk described on the operating model page. We aim to reply within 1 working day for accessibility problems.

Enforcement procedure

For public sector websites, the Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC) is responsible for enforcing the accessibility regulations in Great Britain, and the Equality Commission for Northern Ireland (ECNI) in Northern Ireland. If you're not happy with how a public body responds to your complaint, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service (EASS), or the ECNI if you live in Northern Ireland.

How we tested

Every page is checked automatically on each build in a real browser, at desktop width in light mode and phone width in dark mode, for: script errors, sideways scrolling, controls without labels, buttons and links without names, duplicate ids, skipped heading levels, text contrast (4.5:1, or 3:1 for large text) and hidden panels that show by mistake. Guided tours are tested for keyboard focus and closing with Escape. The front page carousel is tested with and without reduced motion.

Automated checks find only some problems. Testing with people who use assistive technology is part of the user research plan (XDMH-212).

Privacy notice

Status: the Commons is a prototype. Before it is offered as a public service, the organisation that runs it will be named here as the data controller, with its contact details and those of its data protection officer, and a data protection impact assessment will be completed.

What we collect

  • Data you check: nothing. Files you open in the checker, assistant or reconciliation tool are processed in your browser and are not sent anywhere.
  • Things you choose to share: contributions, wishlist suggestions and votes, survey answers, help requests and media submissions. These are linked to an anonymous id from your sign-in, never to your name or email.
  • Contact details: only if you add one to a help request. We use it only to reply, and delete it within 12 months.
  • AI assistant: your messages and the column names of a file you load are sent to the AI to answer you. Example values are sent only if you tick the box. Messages that look like they include personal data are blocked.
  • Your browser: some choices (your role, learning progress, maturity answers, chosen clauses) are kept in your own browser so you don't lose them. You can clear them at any time in your browser settings.

How it's shown

  • Survey results are published only as totals. Any total under 5 is hidden. Free-text answers are never published without moderation.
  • Contributions are checked by a moderator before they're public.
  • We don't use tracking cookies or analytics.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete anything you have shared. Use Ask the Commons for help. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
